# Electrochemistry Lab

Interactive educational website with three experiments:

1. **Movement of Charges:** visualize electrons in the external circuit and K⁺ / NO₃⁻ migration through the salt bridge of a Zn–Cu voltaic cell; open the circuit or remove the salt bridge to see how the current changes.
2. **Voltaic Cell:** complete the Zn–Cu circuit, power the load, and explore the spontaneous oxidation/reduction half-reactions.
3. **Electrolytic Cell:** turn on a DC power supply to split acidified water into hydrogen and oxygen. View the half-equations and 2:1 gas production ratio.

The diagrams are explanatory models; movement rates, bubble sizes and reaction animations are illustrative rather than quantitative measurements.

## Run / publish

Open `index.html` in a modern browser, keeping `styles.css` and `app.js` alongside it.

For GitHub Pages: upload `index.html`, `styles.css`, and `app.js` to the root of a repository; then choose **Settings → Pages → Deploy from a branch → main → /(root)**. The website will be available at your repository's Pages URL.

## Scientific references

- [OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Galvanic Cells](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/17-2-galvanic-cells)
- [OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Electrode and Cell Potentials](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/17-3-electrode-and-cell-potentials)
- [OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Electrolysis](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/17-7-electrolysis)

The voltaic model uses the Zn(s)/Zn²⁺(aq) and Cu²⁺(aq)/Cu(s) half-cells with a KNO₃ salt bridge. The standard potential is approximately +1.10 V. In the water-electrolysis model, an external DC supply drives a non-spontaneous reaction with inert electrodes and acidified water, yielding hydrogen at the cathode and oxygen at the anode.
